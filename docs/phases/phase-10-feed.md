## What Phase 3 already provides

*Added 2026-09-09, when Phase 3 completed. Check these before building — the most common way to
waste a phase is to rebuild something the previous one shipped.*

- **The feed is seeded and every post is generated** — 65 posts, none hand-written, plus 463
  reactions, comments and saves, and 179 follows.
- **The single-anchor rule is a database check constraint** (ADR-006), not a convention. A post with
  zero or two anchors cannot be inserted. `db:verify` also asserts no post has
  `source <> 'GENERATED'`, which is what keeps the feed from becoming status updates.
- **Counters are denormalised** (`reactionCount`, `commentCount`, `saveCount`) and
  `db:verify` asserts they match the rows behind them. Update them in the same transaction.
- **`Follow` is polymorphic over USER · PROJECT · TOPIC · COLLEGE · GROUP with no foreign key** —
  referential integrity is asserted, not constrained. Do not add an FK.
- **`Reaction` kinds are academic signals** — `INSIGHTFUL`, `USEFUL`, `IMPRESSIVE`,
  `SUPPORT`. Not a like button, and better ranking input.

## What Phase 6 already provides

*Added 2026-09-09, when Phase 6 completed.*

- **`lib/follow.ts` is built and tested** — `toggleFollow`, `followCounts`, `listFollowers`
  (respecting each follower's own privacy) and `suggestedFollows`, which is deterministic and
  carries the reason for every suggestion.
- **The follow *control* is yours.** It was left unbuilt on purpose: an optimistic button with
  nowhere to appear is not a deliverable.
- **Follow counts are computed live.** Denormalising them is your call once the read volume is real
  — a cached counter that can drift is worse than a cheap query.
- **The profile's activity section is waiting for you**, and `CONNECTIONS` visibility needs a
  settled answer to "is a follow a connection" before it can mean anything.

## What Phase 7 already provides

*Added 2026-09-10, when Phase 7 completed.*

- **Two things were deliberately deferred to this phase, and are the first things to build:**
  **unread indicators** on `/groups` and **"request to join"** for a `REQUEST`-policy group. Both
  are notifications, and half-building a notification in Phase 7 would have put the delivery
  decision in two places.
- **`Mention` rows are already written** by `replyToThread` — the evidence is captured, nothing
  delivers it yet. Turning one into a notification is this phase's job.
- **Mentions resolve against the group's members, never the college directory**
  (`lib/workspace/mentions.ts`). That is a privacy rule: an `@mention` that reaches somebody
  outside the room would leak the room, and an "unknown user" error would confirm a username exists.
  Keep the same rule for any surface you add.
- **`listActivity()` in `queries/workspace.ts`** already interleaves `LedgerEvent` and
  `ActivityEvent` for a group. The feed is a different scope over the same idea.
- **The feed is anchored, never free-form** (the Phase 1 differentiator). Every workspace event
  already points at a real row, so there is a real anchor available for each one.

## Objective

Build the social layer **the right way**: a feed that cannot be empty, because it is generated by
the product being used, and that cannot become noise, because every post is anchored to something
real (ADR-006).

## In scope

- The anchored post model and typed composer
- Generated posts from domain events
- Three feed tabs with deterministic ranking
- Engagement: like, comment, save, share
- The notification system with SSE delivery
- Notification preferences and the email digest

## Out of scope

- AI-generated summaries or ranking (Phase 18) · Direct messaging (out of scope for MVP)

## Deliverables

### The post model
- [ ] **Every post references exactly one anchor** — project, idea, question or resource — enforced
      by the Phase 3 database check constraint
- [ ] `source`: `AUTHORED` (a person wrote it) or `GENERATED` (a domain event produced it)
- [ ] Post types, each with its own fields and its own card layout:
      - `PROJECT_UPDATE` — progress, what changed, optional media
      - `MILESTONE` — generated on milestone close
      - `RESEARCH_FINDING` — finding, method, source
      - `PROTOTYPE_DEMO` — description, media, links
      - `ACHIEVEMENT` — what, where, who
      - `QUESTION` — question, context, tags, accepted answer
      - `RESOURCE` — link, why it is useful, topic
      - `PROJECT_PUBLISHED` — generated on publication
      - `IDEA_POSTED` — generated from the Idea Hub
- [ ] Visibility per post: `PUBLIC · COLLEGE · FOLLOWERS · GROUP`, resolved through
      `resolveVisibility()`

### The composer
- [ ] Type chooser first, **then** the fields for that type — not a free text box with a dropdown.
      The type determines the form, which is what keeps content structured.
- [ ] Anchor selection is required and cannot be skipped
- [ ] Rich text (Tiptap), server-sanitised, with `@mentions` and `#topic` tags
- [ ] Attach: images (converted or size-capped), files from a group library, links with a preview
      card generated server-side
- [ ] Draft autosave; a lost post is a lost user
- [ ] Guidance text per type — the same blank-page principle as Phase 8's section prompts

### Generated posts
- [ ] `lib/feed/generate.ts` — domain events create posts through the **same** `createPost()` path
      as authored ones, so there is one code path and one set of rules
- [ ] Triggers: milestone closed · project published · prototype uploaded · attestation issued ·
      project archived · idea reaches Looking for Team
- [ ] Attributed to the group, naming the acting member
- [ ] A group setting to suppress generated posts, for groups that would rather stay quiet

### The feed — `/feed`
- [ ] Three tabs: **Following** · **My College** · **Discover**
- [ ] `lib/feed/ranking.ts` — deterministic, unit-tested scoring:
      - Recency decay (half-life in config)
      - Engagement (reactions, comments, saves, weighted)
      - Affinity (following the author / project / topic / college)
      - Type weight (a milestone outranks a resource share)
      - **A diversity penalty**, so one active group cannot flood a feed
- [ ] Ranking is computed in SQL, not in JavaScript over a fetched superset (see
      `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`)
- [ ] Cursor pagination; the first page server-rendered, later pages via a server action
- [ ] Filter by post type and topic
- [ ] Empty state that is genuinely useful: suggested people, projects and topics to follow

### Engagement
- [ ] Reactions — a small fixed set with meaning, not a like button:
      **Insightful · Useful · Impressive · Support**
- [ ] Comments with one level of replies, mentions and reactions
- [ ] Save to a personal collection; `/saved` with folders
- [ ] Share: copy link, and a share to a group workspace
- [ ] Report, feeding the Phase 16 moderation queue
- [ ] Counts denormalised on the post row, maintained in the same transaction as the change
- [ ] All engagement optimistic

### Notifications
- [ ] `lib/notifications/dispatch.ts` — one entry point, one template registry
- [ ] Categories: mentions · task assigned · task due · comment on your content · reaction ·
      new follower · group invite · milestone · deadline · submission reviewed · attestation ·
      announcement · collaboration request · system
- [ ] In-app: the bell in `AppShell`, a panel, `/notifications` with filters and mark-all-read
- [ ] **Realtime via SSE** — `GET /api/sse` per user, fed by an in-process emitter behind
      `lib/realtime/` (ADR-005). Reconnect with backoff on drop.
- [ ] Grouping: "3 people reacted to your post", not three rows
- [ ] `/settings/notifications` — per category, per channel (in-app / email), with a quiet-hours
      window
- [ ] Email digest: daily or weekly, one template, generated from unread notifications, with a
      one-click unsubscribe that actually works
- [ ] Deep links: every notification opens exactly the thing it is about

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The feed is populated entirely by generated activity from the seed** — no hand-written posts
   anywhere.
2. A post cannot be created without an anchor, at the API level as well as in the UI.
3. Ranking is deterministic: the same inputs produce the same order, and the unit test asserts it.
4. The diversity penalty prevents one group's five posts from occupying the top five slots.
5. A notification arrives in a second browser **without a refresh**, within two seconds.
6. SSE reconnects automatically after the connection is dropped.
7. Notification preferences are honoured — a disabled category produces nothing, in either channel.
8. The feed's first page server-renders; scrolling loads more without a layout shift.
9. Engagement updates optimistically and reverts visibly on failure.
10. First-load JS for `/feed` is within the authenticated budget in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`.

## Notes & risks

- **The anchor rule is the whole design.** The moment a free-form post is allowed "just for
  flexibility", the feed becomes a status-update wasteland and the product's differentiation is
  gone. The database constraint exists precisely so a future route handler cannot quietly bypass it.
- **Generated posts are what make the feed non-empty on day one.** Prioritise them over the
  composer if time is short — a feed full of real milestone events with no authored posts is far
  better than an empty feed with a beautiful composer.
- **Ranking must be explainable.** When someone asks why they saw something, "recency, your follow
  of that project, and its engagement" is an answer. A model's output is not. This is also why
  ranking stays deterministic until Phase 18, and even then AI only re-ranks a deterministic
  shortlist.
- **SSE and Nginx buffering.** In production, `proxy_buffering off` on `/api/sse` is mandatory or
  notifications arrive in batches. It is in the Phase 17 runbook; know about it before you deploy,
  not during.
- Meaningful reactions beat a like button. "Insightful" and "Useful" are academic signals; a heart
  is not. They are also better ranking inputs.
- Denormalise engagement counts. Counting rows per render does not survive a real feed and it will
  be the first thing that gets slow.
- Email digests need a real unsubscribe. A broken one is a spam complaint and a deliverability
  problem that takes months to recover from.
- Do not build direct messaging. Threaded discussion covers group communication and mentions cover
  the rest; a DM inbox is a large build with a large moderation surface and it is not the MVP.
